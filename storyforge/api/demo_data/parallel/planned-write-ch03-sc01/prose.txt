Act III

The clock on the signal tower face read 18:20, returning the city to the damp chill of the present. Ivo Venn adjusted the bakelite housing of his portable radio. Through the small speaker, sharp static burst in short, erratic pulses. 

"Harbor locks are down," Oren Pike’s voice growled out of the speaker. The captain was at the perimeter gate down the lane. "No transit permitted without council clearance."

Ivo pressed the radio’s selector switch to the main council relay band. He paused, holding his breath as the vacuum tube hummed. He pressed the broadcast button once to transmit a cross-check query to verify the lock order.

"Key code four-zero-nine," the radio receiver chattered back in an automated tone.

Ivo stared at the lit frequency dial. Four-zero-nine was an expired seasonal key code—withdrawn from municipal use two weeks ago. He checked the indicator light on the transmitter casing; the single signal had consumed one of his two remaining power charges. Exactly one transmission charge remained in the internal cell.

He hauled his heavy gear bag over his shoulder and ran. Twelve minutes of sharp footfalls on wet cobblestones brought him from the tower through the sea mist to the archway of the Public archive.

Nadi Vale stepped from the shadows beneath the stone vaulting. Her coat reeked of ozone. "The quay lost main power the second that transfer order clicked over the wire," she said, her voice clipped. "The dock cranes are completely cold."

"The council signal is bad," Ivo said after a careful pause. "It’s running on an expired key."

Oren Pike stepped into the entryway, his boots heavy on the flags. He held a brass bar across the archive door. "My order says secure the perimeter until the relief fund chest passes through."

"I heard the words," Ivo said, looking Oren in the eye. "I need their meaning. The clearance was sent using an old key code. Someone inside the council is broadcasting invalid transfer orders."

Nadi stepped beside Ivo, leaning against the cold stone of the doorway. "If the locks stay shut, the grid dies before dawn."

Oren looked from Ivo’s radio to Nadi’s grease-stained hands. Slowly, the captain lowered the brass bar, stepping back to let them pass into the archive court. 

"I’m holding the perimeter," Oren said quietly. "If the authorization is fraudulent, we take our stand here."

Ivo nodded once, stepping through the doorway. His radio sat heavy in his coat pocket, down to its final charge, but the path inside was open.
